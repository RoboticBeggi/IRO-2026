Wayang kulit is shadow puppet theatre. The best-known Malaysian form comes from Kelantan, on the east coast of Peninsular Malaysia.
Flat puppets are cut from animal hide, carved with fine holes and painted. Their shadows are thrown onto a white cloth screen called the kelir by a lamp behind it.
One master puppeteer, the Tok Dalang, sits behind the screen and moves all the puppets, speaks every voice and narrates the whole story, often for hours.
A small orchestra plays with him: the serunai (a reed oboe), drums called gendang, gongs and small cymbals.
Many stories come from Hikayat Maharaja Wana, the Malay telling of the Indian Ramayana epic: Seri Rama, his wife Siti Dewi, the monkey warrior Hanuman and the demon king Maharaja Wana.
Funny characters such as Pak Dogol and Wak Long make the audience laugh and talk about everyday life.
A leaf-shaped puppet, the pohon beringin (tree of life), opens and closes the show.
Today there are only a few Tok Dalang left, and young people are learning so that the tradition does not disappear.
